## Blind-spot probes

- **Hardest decision:** representing colour as an additive rank under a mod-m glue. It makes closure trivial to prove but makes overlap colour unpredictable to a painter (a+b+1 wraps). The alternative — Orphic Maker's "nearest wheel colour" — is predictable but not a sheaf. Neither was tested with a painter.
- **Rejected alternative:** a real boolean/planar-region engine for overlaps. Rejected for speed; it is the only thing that makes the sheaf claim literal (see #3).
- **Least confident:** the `bind` law. It is non-associative (needed for K_n to matter) but its golden-angle arm uses tree depth, so two bracketings that differ only deep in the tree can look nearly identical. A law whose output visibly depends on bracketing at every level would make the associahedron *legible*, not just present.

## Lesson atom

`correction · spectral-ops` — When an op forwards its params object to a shared filter, the op's defaults must be the **full** parameter contract of that filter, not the subset shown in the UI. Missing keys become `NaN` and fail silently in Float arrays. Check: `Object.keys(filterContract).every(k => k in op.defaults)` at module load.
