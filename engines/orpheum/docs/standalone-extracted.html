Multi-center field

A solid is projected to 2D down a chosen axis — its vertices become centres of rotation woven into one continuous field (not separate mandalas). Coverage grows each centre's rotational field until neighbours overlap and the frame fills in. A rotated overlay reading adds the diffraction.
One continuous geome with many centers of rotation woven edge-to-edge — a wallpaper field. No negative space.
Two chroma-separated planes of the same field; Plane B is offset and composited over black. Plate intensity keeps the dark ground dominant so the diffraction fringe reads as light on black rather than a full-frame wash.

Two planes of view

Two overlapping planes of view. Plane B counter-rotates against A; their geometries overlap into emergent moiré. Works on a single mandala too.

Per-center rotation

Signed degrees — negative = counter-rotation. Applied at the node-class level so global symmetry is preserved exactly.

Animation

Transform-only: planes counter-rotate, orbits spin (like gears), the core breathes. Symmetry holds every frame; Freeze gives a publishable still.

One subunit, centred — no replication. A quieter layer for the journey between dense mandalas.
One subunit tiled edge-to-edge — a flat textile of the motif you pick below. With Stencil cut, cutting a portion opens that window in every tile at once. Pure vector — sharp at any DPI.
A photo or render as a layer. Crop it, place it in perspective, then cut or blend the layers above into it.
Where this layer crosses geometry behind it, the intersection is re-cut as a new flat colour from the palette — opaque plate logic, no transparency. The blend math only picks the mix; the result always snaps to a real palette colour, so the composition stays in its core colours.
Each colour costs a full-plate mask on every render, and the whole stack is capped at 12. Past 4 you pay a lot of memory for overlaps that are mostly slivers.
Pick sub-units and/or rings to cut out of this layer. Because each sub-unit is instanced across the mandala, selecting one knocks out every instance at once — opening a symmetric set of windows onto the layer(s) behind. Or cut by colour to open windows through every shape painted in a chosen palette colour. Keep selected inverts either.
This motif is a stack of portions — ground, form, focal, emblem. Click any portion below to cut it away, opening a window onto the layer(s) behind through exactly that shape.

Slice shift · active layer

Cut this layer where you want it, band by band. Click the plate to drop a cut, drag a cut to move it, Alt-click to remove it. Switch to Shift and drag inside a band to slide it — rows slide ↔, columns slide ↕. Pure vector, so it stays sharp at 450 DPI.
Turn on Layered compositing in the ⧉ Layers panel (4) first — slicing works per layer.
Cuts land on the grid; hold Shift while dragging a band to snap the offset too.
Arrow keys nudge the last band you touched (⇧ for a coarse step). Rows and columns hold separate cut sets — both apply, rows first.

Actions

Print & screen · to 900 DPI

—
Rendered in tiles straight off the vector, each tile rasterised 1:1 at print resolution with the plate's user space scaled to match — so SVG filters, patterns and image crops resolve at full DPI instead of the browser's small default buffers. The pHYs chunk is rewritten (any 72-DPI one removed), so a pre-flight checker reads the true physical size. For canvas, Download SVG is still the sharpest route unless a layer carries an uploaded photo. 4K / 8K screen masters render the long edge at exactly that pixel count — 1:1 device pixels, zero resampling, no pixelation on a UHD display.
