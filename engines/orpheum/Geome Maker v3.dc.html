The method

From form to geome

In his late image-art, Adi Da Samraj worked with named principles of abstraction. The geome: primary geometries — rectangle, circle, triangle — structure indicated by line and color. The linead: the free hand-drawn line, composed into the image. Beyond both, a final stage he called the orphic font. The images are aperspectival — made from no located point of view — and aniconic: nothing represented, everything presented. Their stated purpose: to draw the viewer out of egoic separateness toward prior unity.

The intention

Orphic art transcends the ego; Narcissistic art reflects it. Adi Da: “Narcissus (or the ego-‘I’) must die. Orpheus… must win.” This instrument takes a side — the essay.

Resolved from v2
Blends escaped the palette — alpha compositing produced arbitrary RGB. Overlaps are now painted as chords, palette members by construction, verified live on the canvas.
Blending was raster and lossy. It is now vector clip-intersection; exports stay crisp at any scale.
Works were not reproducible. The engine is pure and seed-deterministic; every work prints as a term.
The symbol space was thin. Twenty typed recipes — ten Jungian, ten hermetic — over three primaries.
Now reconciled with the real v2 source: the authentic core palettes — Spectra prism, the Hermetic deck, and the Sanzo Wada combinations (Jewel, Antique, Indigo, Pastel) — are lyres here, and every one of them chords closed. v2's layer blend modes (multiply, screen) are exactly where color escaped; the chord law replaces them.
